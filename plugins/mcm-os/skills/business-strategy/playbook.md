# Strategy playbook

The general frameworks the strategy session draws on. What's specific to her (her market, her competitors' prices) comes from the research step, not from here. Numbers below are rules of thumb, not laws: say so when using one.

## 1. Pick the first offer
- **One offer first.** Not a ladder, not a menu. The first offer proves people pay her; everything else comes after.
- **The test:** the offer she can deliver well **now**, to people she can reach **now**, that solves a problem they already say out loud.
- **Closest to what already works:** if people already pay her for something (lessons, sessions, consulting), the first offer is that, packaged. Not a new idea.
- **Outcome over topic:** "Run a meeting in English with confidence in 8 weeks" sells; "Business English" doesn't.

## 2. Choose the format
| Format | Her effort | Typical price | Scales? | Good first offer when… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1:1 sessions / package | high, per client | highest per hour | no | she has few buyers and wants cash and learning fast |
| Live small group / cohort | medium, fixed per round | mid | some | she wants to test the curriculum with real students |
| Workshop (one session) | low | low | yes | she needs a first yes and a list |
| Recorded course | high up front, low after | low to mid | yes | the curriculum is already proven live |
| Membership / community | ongoing | low monthly | yes | she already has an engaged audience |

Default recommendation for someone without a proven curriculum: **a live small-group cohort first**, recorded later from what worked live.

## 3. Set the price
- Anchor on the **outcome** and on the **research** (what similar offers charge), never on her hours.
- Never the cheapest in the research. Cheapest attracts the buyers who value it least.
- A **founding price** for the first round is fine, said as such ("founding students: $X; next round: $Y"), with a reason (they help shape it).
- Price is a strategy decision, revisited after the first round, not a forever number.

## 4. Sell before you build
- Build only what people already paid for. Validate with one of:
  - **Waitlist** with a short reason to join (weakest signal).
  - **Presale with payment or deposit** (strongest signal).
  - **Pilot cohort**: sell the first round, build each week just ahead of the students.
- A rule of thumb for "it's real": a handful of paid presales (5-10) from people who aren't friends or family.
- No presales after an honest push: change the offer, the promise or the audience, not the slides.

## 5. Launch order
1. Warm audience first: people who already know her (past students, list, DMs).
2. Content that names the problem, then the offer, with a window and a real deadline.
3. Deliver the first round; collect results and words from students.
4. Only then: recorded version, higher price, wider audience, ads.

## 6. The path to a sale
How someone goes from finding her to paying, in her words, in 3-5 steps (e.g. post → free workshop → call → payment). Each step names what happens there and what the next step is. If a step needs a tool, it lives in MCM (pages, calendar, payments, courses).

## 7. 90-day goals
- **One number.** Paid students, presales, revenue: one, with a date.
- **One #1 priority.** Everything else is "after this".
- Anti-goals are as useful as goals: what she won't do in these 90 days.

## 8. When to revisit
- After the first round of the first offer.
- When the 90 days end.
- When something big changes (time, money, a new opportunity).
