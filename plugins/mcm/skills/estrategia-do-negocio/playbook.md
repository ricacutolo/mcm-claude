# Strategy playbook

The general frameworks and benchmarks the strategy session draws on. What's specific to her (her market, her competitors' prices) comes from the research step, not from here. Every number below names its source in brackets; most come from course platforms that sell to course creators, so treat them as signals, not laws, and say so when using one. Read the section in play, not all of it.

## 1. Pick the first offer
- **One offer first.** Not a ladder, not a menu. The first offer proves people pay her; the rest comes after.
- **The test:** what she can deliver well **now**, to people she can reach **now**, solving a problem they already say out loud.
- **Closest to what already works:** if people already pay her for something (lessons, sessions, consulting), the first offer is that, packaged. Not a new idea.
- **Outcome over topic:** "Run a meeting in English with confidence in 8 weeks" sells; "Business English" doesn't.
- **Teachable without prep:** a topic she could teach for 30 minutes without preparing is a good sign she can deliver it [Welsh].

## 2. Make the offer worth it (the value equation)
Value goes up with the **dream outcome** and the **perceived likelihood** of getting it, and down with the **time it takes** and the **effort it costs** the buyer [Hormozi, $100M Offers]. Use it as four questions about her offer:
1. What result does the buyer actually want, in their words?
2. What makes them believe it will work for them (her track record, students' results, a guarantee, live support)?
3. How fast do they see a first win (week 1, not week 8)?
4. What does she take off their plate (templates, feedback, done-with-you parts)?

Bonuses that are real parts of the work, named separately, widen the gap between price and value. A guarantee strengthens a good offer; it never rescues a weak one [Hormozi].

## 3. Choose the format
| Format | Her effort | Typical price [source] | Completion | Good first offer when… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1:1 package | high, per client | highest per hour | — | she's new to the niche and wants cash and learning fast [Kajabi] |
| Live cohort / group (12-25 people, 8-12 weeks) | medium, per round | $995-3,500 [Ruzuku bands] | 53% scheduled vs 42-43% self-paced; 65% with 11-25 students [Ruzuku, 1.3M enrollments] | she wants to test the curriculum with real students |
| Hybrid (recorded + weekly live Q&A) | medium | $395-1,250 [Ruzuku bands] | higher than self-paced [Ruzuku] | some material exists, live support carries results |
| Workshop / bootcamp (1 day to 2 weeks) | low | low | — | she needs a first yes and a list |
| Recorded course | high up front, low after | median $150; middle half $49-357 [Ruzuku, 11,691 courses]; first course median $89 [Podia, 2021] | ~42% [Ruzuku] | the curriculum is already proven live |
| Membership / community | ongoing | low monthly | courses with community complete more [Learning Revolution, unsourced] | she already has an engaged audience |
| Mastermind / high-touch | high | $2,000-5,000+ [Ruzuku bands] | — | she has proven results and senior buyers |

The price bands are Ruzuku's illustrations, not measured medians. Completion figures are observational: they don't prove the format caused the result [Ruzuku].

**Default recommendation** for someone without a proven curriculum: **a live small-group cohort first** (8-12 weeks, up to ~25 people), recorded later from what worked live. 1:1 first instead when she has no audience yet: easier to fill, fastest learning [Kajabi]. Later, a common shape is group as the core with 1:1 as a premium tier [Kajabi].

## 4. Set the price
- Anchor on the **outcome** and on the **research** (what close offers charge), never on her hours.
- **Don't start cheap.** Most recorded courses sell under ~$350 [Podia, 89% of ~132k sales], and very low prices hurt perceived value and need far more buyers for the same revenue [Podia]. Live, outcome-driven programs sit well above that [Ruzuku].
- Business & professional courses: median $279, middle half $60-997 [Ruzuku, n=603].
- **Founding price** for the first round, said as such, with a reason (they help shape it) and an end date. Presell guides use discounts around 40% plus perks like input on the content or extra live time [SchoolMaker].
- **Payment plans** from about $99 up [Podia]; about one creator in five offers them [Podia].
- Price is a strategy decision, revisited after the first round, not a forever number.

## 5. Sell before you build
- Build only what people already paid for. Ways to validate, weakest to strongest:
  1. **Waitlist** with a reason to join. Pre-launch pages often convert 20-40% of warm visitors to sign-ups [LaunchList, unsourced]; sign-ups are interest, not money.
  2. **Presale** with full payment or a deposit, and a clear refund if it doesn't run [SchoolMaker].
  3. **Pilot cohort:** sell the first round, build each week just ahead of the students, one module a week [SchoolMaker].
- **Before the presale:** an outline and the first module, no more [SchoolMaker].
- **Run it 2-4 weeks** [SchoolMaker]; one simple rhythm is announcement, reminder, last call [Welsh].
- **Decide the bar before you start:** the number of buyers that covers her time and costs. Below it, the round doesn't run and buyers get refunded [SchoolMaker]. As a rough guide, a handful of paid buyers who aren't friends or family is a real signal.
- No presales after an honest push: change the offer, the promise or the audience, not the slides.

## 6. Launch order
1. **Warm audience first:** people who already know her (past students, list, DMs, communities she's in).
2. **Open-close launch:** content that names the problem, then the offer, with a window and a real deadline.
3. **Deliver the first round**; collect results and students' own words.
4. **Then:** recorded or hybrid version, higher price, wider audience, ads, evergreen.

**What to expect from a list:** launch email sequences to an existing list often convert around 2-5% of it to buyers [industry rule of thumb, no named study]. Use it to size the goal: 10 buyers needs a few hundred warm people, not 30.

## 7. The path to a sale
How someone goes from finding her to paying, in her words, in 3-5 steps (e.g. post → free workshop → call → payment). Each step says what happens there and what the next step is. The tools for each step live in MCM (pages, calendar, payments, courses, community).

## 8. Guarantee
- **None** is fine for a small founding round when the refund rule is clear.
- **Conditional:** money back if she did the work (attended, submitted) and didn't get the result.
- **Unconditional:** money back within N days, no questions. Strongest signal; only with a strong offer [Hormozi].
- Write it in one line, and only one she can keep.

## 9. 90-day goals
- **One number.** Paid students, presales or revenue: one, with a date.
- **One #1 priority.** Everything else is "after this".
- Anti-goals are as useful as goals: what she won't do in these 90 days.

## 10. When to revisit
- After the first round of the first offer.
- When the 90 days end.
- When something big changes (time, money, a new opportunity).
