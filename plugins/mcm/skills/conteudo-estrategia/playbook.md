# Copy playbook

How posts get written. MCM's craft, shared by every business that uses the content machine. It ships with the plugin and is **read-only**: no skill ever edits this file. What works for her business is recorded in her own files (`departamentos/conteudo/notas-da-biblioteca.md`, `departamentos/conteudo/swipe.md`), by conteudo-revisao on her sim.

Her own voice lives in `sobre-mim/escrita.md`. This file is the craft that sits on top of the voice.

This edition's posts are written in Brazilian Portuguese. The examples below are English; carry the move, never the words, and never translate a hook word for word.

Status tags, same as the strategy library: **unproven** (from a reference or one post), **working** (held up across several posts), **dropped** (tried, didn't work). Nothing is **working** without real numbers.

---

## 1. Rules (always)

- Lead with what the reader gets, never with how it works. Features become what they mean for the reader's day.
- Never technical: no "CRM", no tool names, no jargon. Write for people who don't care how it works.
- It's about sales and their time, not marketing.
- Real things only. No invented numbers, results or stances. A number in a post is the reader's own ("what one lost job costs you"), never a claim of ours.
- Speak at the reader's level. If they wouldn't say the word out loud, don't write it.
- One idea per post.
- Every line earns the next one (Sugarman's slippery slide). If a line can go, it goes.
- Numerals, always (3 mistakes, not three mistakes). No emoji.

## 2. Awareness levels (Schwartz) — where the reader is

Write to where the reader already is, not where we want them to be.

| Level | The reader... | What the post does | Fits post types |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | doesn't know they have a problem | names the problem in their words | Leak, Story, Reader's own math |
| Problem aware | feels the pain, no fix in mind | shows the pain is fixable | 3 mistakes, Myth vs truth, Quick win, Before and after |
| Solution aware | knows fixes exist | shows why this way works | How it works, Old way vs new way, Question you always get |
| Product aware | knows her | gives reasons to trust | Who we are, Proof, Behind the scenes |
| Most aware | ready | makes the offer plain | What you get |

Which levels to write to right now follows the layers that are on in `departamentos/conteudo/estrategia.md`.

## 3. Hooks

The first line or slide does most of the work. Pick the pattern that fits the topic and post type. Adapted from Nuno Tavares's 11 hook patterns (reference only); the "specific number" patterns use the reader's numbers, never invented ones. The examples come from a business that sets up lead follow-up for service businesses; write hers from her own world.

| # | Pattern | Shape | Example | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reframe | A common belief, flipped in one line | "You don't need more leads. You need to stop losing the ones you get." | unproven |
| 2 | Contrast | Group A does X. Group B does Y. | "Busy owners answer when they can. Booked-out owners answer right away." | unproven |
| 3 | The reader's number | A number they can check themselves | "Count the calls you missed last week." | unproven |
| 4 | Question | A specific question they answer in their head | "What happens the moment someone fills out your form?" | unproven |
| 5 | Real moment | A true moment, no persona | "I rebuilt a client's whole system because it was too big for her." | unproven |
| 6 | Contrarian | The unpopular position, stated plainly | "More automations won't fix your follow-up." | unproven |
| 7 | Pain list | 2-3 pains they recognize | "Texts, DMs, sticky notes. Something always slips." | unproven |
| 8 | Behind the scenes | "Here's what nobody tells you about..." | "Here's what nobody tells you about missed calls." | unproven |
| 9 | Receipts | "I did X for Y. Here's what happened." (real only) | "I set up the same thing first for every client. Here's why." | unproven |
| 10 | Reverse | "Most people think X. Here's why that's wrong." | "Most owners think happy clients leave reviews on their own." | unproven |
| 11 | Stolen lesson | "I tried what X does. Here's what happened." (real only) | — | unproven |

## 4. Frameworks (reference)

| Framework | Shape | Use for | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem, agitate, solve | Name the pain, show what it costs, give the fix | Leak, 3 mistakes | classic direct response |
| Before, after, bridge | Their day now, their day after, what gets them there | Before and after, Old way vs new way | classic direct response |
| What they get | Feature, then what it means, then what it means for their day | every post | Jim Edwards |
| Value equation | Bigger result, more likely, faster, less effort | What you get, the offer | Alex Hormozi |
| Slippery slide | Each line's only job is to get the next one read | every post | Joseph Sugarman |

## 5. Structure per post type

### Tips list carousel (5 slides) — Leak, 3 mistakes, Quick win, How it works
1. Hook: the words that sting in the accent color. "Swipe through →".
2-4. One point each: number, short title, 2-4 lines; the turn (what it costs or what changes) in the accent color.
5. Reframe line, the promise in the accent color, the slot's ending (§ 7).

Other post types get their structure when their template is built.

## 6. Caption formula

1. Hook line (a different angle from slide 1, not a repeat).
2. The plain explanation, 2-3 short paragraphs, what they get.
3. The wedge: why customers pick her, in one line (from `estrategia.md`).
4. The slot's ending (§ 7): the ask, a light invitation, or nothing.
5. 3-5 hashtags.

This is the main caption, used by the platforms that share it (Instagram, Facebook). A platform whose file in `platforms/` gives it its own caption (LinkedIn) follows that file instead.

## 7. How posts end

Not every post asks for something. Her strategy sets the rhythm (Como os posts terminam) and the plan marks each slot `pedido`, `leve` or `nenhum`. Asking on every post reads as selling all the time; most posts earn trust by just being useful.

**The ask**, by layer, on the slots marked `pedido` (about 1 in 3 by default):

| Layer | The ask |
|---|---|
| Credibility | Book a call, or message me (in her words). Link in bio. |
| Audience | Follow for more. |
| Leads | Comment a word to get X; DM me; the lead magnet. |

**Light endings**, on the slots marked `leve: <qual>`: save it for later · a question to answer in the comments (a real one, from their own experience) · share it with someone who needs it.

**None**, on the slots marked `nenhum`: the last line is the point itself. No invitation, no sign-off ask.

## 8. The check before the copy gate

Every draft passes these 4 before it's shown:
1. **Hook:** specific, about them, makes the next line necessary.
2. **What they get:** the reader's outcome is clear by slide 2.
3. **Plain:** no technical word, no tool name, no claim we can't back.
4. **Ending:** matches the slot: the ask (one, clear, for the layer), the light ending named, or none.

## 11. Newsletter

For an email to her list (`platforms/newsletter.md` has the format and scheduling rules). The rules in section 1 and the 4-point check apply; what changes:
- **Her material first.** The email is built from what she gave: a few lines, a story, a question someone asked. Claude shapes it; it doesn't replace it.
- **One idea, one ask.** If two ideas fight for the email, the second one is next issue.
- **Open in the moment.** First line is a scene, a question or a sentence she said, not "I hope this finds you well" or "This week I want to talk about".
- **Write like a letter to one person.** "You", short paragraphs, her voice; no headers, no bullet lists unless the format needs one.
- **The ask by layer:** credibility → reply with your question / book a call; audience → forward this to someone who needs it; leads → the lead magnet or "reply with <word>". On an offer issue the ask is the offer, said plainly, once.
- **Subject last:** 2-3 options, short and specific, no emoji, nothing the email doesn't deliver.

## 12. From the anchor to everything

When an anchor leads the cycle (her strategy's `Anchor:` line: the newsletter or the podcast), the cycle's posts are cut from the approved anchor:
- **One angle per post.** Never the whole piece shrunk down. Someone who never reads the newsletter or hears the episode gets the full point of the post.
- **Which part fits which post type:** its points or steps → a carousel; its story → a reel or a story post; the question it answers → a text post or a client-question post; its moment or scene → a photo post; its before and after → an illustrated carousel.
- **From an episode, too:** a line she said that lands → a quote card or a headline photo, in her exact words; its best 30-60 seconds → a clip pick (a reel when she also recorded video; audio-only clips wait for a later build); its idea → the newsletter, when the newsletter is the podcast.
- **The newsletter from an episode** is the episode's idea written as a letter: worth reading for someone who never listens, never show notes or a bare link. The link to listen goes once, near the end.
- **Say it fresh.** New hook, the post type's own structure (section 5), the platform's caption rules. Reuse her phrases where they're strong; don't paste paragraphs.
- **The anchor mention is rare.** "The full story's in my newsletter" or "it's in this week's episode" only on the leads layer (or the audience layer for the podcast), when there's a link, and not on every post in the cycle.
- **No anchor:** posts come from the plan's topics and her ideas, as always. **Independent shows** (a newsletter and a podcast that don't feed each other): each stands alone, and only the anchor, if one is set, feeds the posts.

## 13. Podcast

For her show (`platforms/podcast.md` has the account, the kit and the audio spec). The rules in section 1 apply; what changes:
- **What it does for her business.** A podcast builds trust faster than posts: people spend 20 minutes with her voice. It rarely grows on its own: most people find a show through YouTube and social, not the podcast apps (Sounds Profitable, 2026: 40% found their favorite show on YouTube, 61% on YouTube or social). So the posts cut from each episode are half the job.
- **Talk from points, never a script.** 3-5 talking points and a hook. Read words sound read.
- **Episode shape:** the hook in the first 30 seconds (what they'll get, a question, a moment), a short intro (who she is in one line, the show's promise), one idea, the ask once (§ 7, by the slot's ending), an outro that points to the next episode. No long music intro, no sponsor talk.
- **One idea per episode.** If two ideas fight, the second is next episode.
- **Length:** what the idea needs, kept about the same every episode. 20-40 minutes is the most common; for a solo teaching show, 15-30 is plenty.
- **Cadence and seasons:** the schedule she can keep beats the ideal one. Every two weeks is the default for a new show; weekly once it's easy. **Seasons** of 6-12 episodes (8 by default), then a planned break with a date to return: easier to keep, easier to binge, natural moments to promote. Same weekday every time.
- **Titles:** what someone would search for or want to hear, specific, no episode number (it has its own field), no clickbait. Up to 150 characters; shorter reads better.
- **Description:** the first two lines carry it: who it's for and what they get. Plain text. Then what's inside, the one ask with its link, the chapters.
- **Chapters:** where each talking point starts, `(00:00) Title`, at least 3, short titles.
- **The trailer:** 1-2 minutes before episode 1: who it's for, what they'll get, how often.
- **The ask by layer:** credibility → book a call / message me; audience → follow the show and share this episode with one person; leads → the lead magnet. Said once, near the end, in her words.
- **Sound:** a quiet room with soft things around, the phone or mic a hand's width away, the same spot each time. A phone is fine to start. A stumble is fine: real beats polished.
- **Guests** later: an interview show needs booking, prep and permission. Start solo; guests are a second-season idea.
- **Judge by the season, not the episode.** Track the posts from each episode, replies and calls she mentions; plays come from her host.

## 14. Newsletter and podcast together

When both are on, the strategy picks how they relate, once:
- **The newsletter is the podcast:** she records; the episode is the anchor; the issue is its idea as a letter, with the link to listen. Best for someone who talks more easily than she writes.
- **The podcast is the newsletter:** she writes; the issue is the anchor; the episode is her talking it through, with what didn't fit in the email. Best for a writer; almost no extra work.
- **Independent:** two shows on their own schedules, the same theme or not (an interview show beside a letter). Neither feeds the other; the anchor is one of them or none.
